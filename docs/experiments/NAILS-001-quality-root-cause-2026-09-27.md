# Nails appearance quality: implementation diagnosis

**Status: VERIFIED for the inspected code and Hand A; broader user-photo quality NEEDS VERIFICATION.** This is an analysis. No model, route, checkpoint, dataset, or compositor was changed.

## Evidence inspected

- Live Hand A outputs: `data/nails/work/live-smoke/hand_a-*.png`, generated through `/nails/generate`. Red and Black are recognizable, but the plate boundaries look like colored, uneven islands; the thumb is especially narrow. French Tip is a thin straight stripe. Previous pixel QA found zero changes outside the hard masks. The available live ZIP contains one hand only.
- Step-50 held-out sheet: `docs/experiments/NAILS-001-LOCAL-v1-step050-review/classic_red.jpg` and the other four style sheets. The approved target already has coarse colored edges. Base often draws a more convincing polished nail on a *regenerated* fingertip; the adapter produces softer color and the final composite clips it to the original mask. Black improves target MAE; Red improvement is marginal. The step-50 adapter saw 50/340 localized train pairs, including 7 Red and 11 Black, so its quality is also exposure-limited. See the [checkpoint evaluation](NAILS-001-LOCAL-v1-step050-evaluation.md).
- Actual code path: `HybridNailsPipeline` in `backend/app/nails/hybrid.py`, `render_target` in `backend/app/nails/render.py`, YOLO subprocess in `backend/app/nails/segmentation.py` and `scripts/nails_segment_once.py`, crop mapping in `backend/app/nails/localized.py`, and final alpha in `backend/app/nails/geometry.py`.
- Hand A's source in `Hands-original.zip` is 1600×1200, whereas the frozen DATA-N001 input and live normalized hand are 512×512. Its five 512-pixel mask islands are only 14–26 pixels tall and 20–29 pixels wide; the combined nail mask is 2,135 pixels, 0.814% of the square image. The localized crop enlarges a roughly 56–70-pixel side to 512 for FLUX and reduces it again for reconstruction. The prior full-hand training signal mean was 0.793%; localized training mean was 11.252% of the crop.
- Bounded offline check on the *original* Hand A photo with the unchanged local `mnemic/nails_seg_s_yolov8_v1.pt`, confidence 0.25, CPU and `retina_masks=True`: `imgsz=640` found five masks totaling 20,195 pixels; `imgsz=1280` found five totaling 19,087 pixels. The overlays are `data/nails/work/quality-audit-20260927/hand0000000_overlay_640.jpg` and `hand0000000_overlay_1280.jpg`. Both still show polygonal boundaries and a very thin/under-covered thumb. This is one-image evidence, not a benchmark. The mask files are in the same directory.

## Root cause, ranked

1. **Plate geometry is represented by a coarse hard mask.** The application segments the already reduced 512 image. The segmenter converts predicted masks to `masks.xy` polygons and fills them into binary pixels. It does no image-edge/cuticle refinement. `validate_nail_mask` checks 3–5 components and proximity to finger landmarks, but cannot establish that a boundary follows the visible cuticle or sidewalls. On Hand A the thumb mask covers only a narrow strip. The final compositor deliberately accepts generated pixels only within this same mask and feathers *inward*. This strongly preserves skin/background, but it also makes an inaccurate plate outline authoritative. A perfect crop generator would still be clipped to that outline.
2. **The training target and production renderer encode synthetic polish rather than photographed material.** DATA-N001 targets were made by `render_target` with fixed RGB colors, a clipped luminance adjustment, an analytic glint and an alpha confined to the mask. The held-out approved targets visibly contain flat fill and angular cuticle edges. French Tip is a threshold on the projected finger direction, not a curved distal smile line; Ombre is a linear fixed-palette interpolation. The LoRA is being asked to match those synthetic targets. More steps with the same targets cannot teach photographic cuticle detail that is absent from them.
3. **Resolution and repeated transforms discard real detail.** The 1600×1200 source becomes 512 before detection/rendering. A 20–29-pixel nail is rotated, enlarged to 512 for the model, then shrunken, inverse-rotated, composited at 512, enlarged to the upload size and composited again. Bicubic resampling cannot recover the original nail's high-frequency grooves, highlights or accurate border. A larger YOLO input size on the original photo improves available spatial detail but did not by itself produce accurate boundaries in the one-image check.
4. **Step-50 model quality is limited independently.** The paired edit/reference path was audited previously; no reference/target swap or missing reference conditioning was found. The adapter had less than one pass over its localized pairs, and Red was barely better than Base on held-out target MAE. Raw Base can look glossy but may reshape the fingertip. The hard-mask composite correctly prevents that geometry drift while exposing the mask/target limitations. This is not evidence that FLUX cannot edit nails; it is evidence that the current adapter and target representation do not yield reliably realistic plates.

**Not the primary cause:** The live route loaded the verified step-50 adapter, and pixel checks confirm the compositor preserves outside-mask pixels. The flat appearance occurs before and at the mask boundary. The current implementation does not support nail extensions/shape change; the original geometry must remain.

## Smallest practical quality path

Keep the current single API, style routing, FLUX Base/step-50 checkpoint, isolated segmenter, and nail-only compositor. Change the quality of the *inputs to compositing* in bounded stages:

1. **Establish the attainable ceiling on two real hands.** Draw accurate high-resolution plate masks for their 10 visible nails as a diagnostic ground truth. Compare current output with exactly the same rendered/model output under the corrected mask. This isolates mask error from style/material error. Do not add these hands to training or change the approved dataset.
2. **Segment nail ROIs at source resolution.** Keep MediaPipe for hand/finger location, but use original-image fingertip regions for the existing YOLO segmenter. Preserve a soft/probability edge or derive a subpixel antialiased edge rather than round-tripping through `masks.xy` and `fillPoly`. Constrain each prediction to one fingertip, check individual plate size/shape/cuticle coverage, and return a retake response when the boundary is unreliable. Test this first; the one-image high-resolution run shows that higher `imgsz` alone is insufficient. If the same checkpoint fails on a small manually outlined review set, a *lightweight nail-segmenter fine-tune* becomes a justified separate proposal, subject to license and validation review.
3. **Composite at original resolution.** Keep FLUX's 512 crop and existing inverse geometry for Red/Black, but map the proposal into the original-resolution nail ROI and apply the refined original-resolution plate alpha there. Avoid making the 512 binary mask the final edge authority. Preserve exact original pixels outside the validated plate. Use narrow edge antialiasing and a small natural cuticle inset; reject masks that would paint skin. This is an incremental change within the current crop/compositor design, not a global FLUX edit.
4. **Use the original nail's photographic structure as the material base.** For Nude/French/Ombre, update the shared DATA-N001 renderer logic to change nail chroma/color while preserving local luminance variation, fine texture and real highlights from the high-resolution source. Replace the canned glint with illumination-aware highlights. Derive French Tip from each plate's distal edge and curved width profile; apply Ombre along the measured base-to-tip axis. Compare with the approved palette and do not alter skin or nail shape. For Red/Black, continue to use the step-50 model as the style proposal, but judge its nail-region result against a source-texture-preserving blend rather than pasting its low-resolution RGB blindly.
5. **Only if that ceiling remains poor, propose a new model/data experiment.** A later LoRA would need photo-realistic paired nail targets and more exposure, not simply more steps on the current flat targets. No retraining is authorized in this analysis.

## Decision gate for the next implementation task

Use the same two hands and all five styles for an A/B sheet: original, current mask, carefully outlined mask, current result, refined-mask result, material-render result. Review close-ups and full hands. Require each visible nail to be covered, no colored skin, plausible cuticle/sidewall/tip, recognizable style, preserved hand/background, and no new nail shape. Measure mask boundary agreement against the diagnostic outlines and retain outside-mask pixel equality. If refined masks alone fix most of the issue, prioritize the segmenter. If masks fix alignment but the plates remain flat, prioritize the material renderer/model-output blend. If even high-quality source images and masks cannot produce convincing material, seek Supervisor approval for improved paired targets before any retraining.
